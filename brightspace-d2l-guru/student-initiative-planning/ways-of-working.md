# Ways of working

## Cadence

- **Weekly sync**, 30 min, same slot. Cancel it if there is nothing to decide.
- **Build check in**, 15 min mid week, only while something is being built
- **Lunch with John**, roughly monthly. Agenda the day before, recap same day.
- **Handoff**, last two weeks of term

Async by default in between.

## Roles

- **Lead** runs syncs, owns the John and manager relationships, breaks ties, owns handoff
- **Idea owner** whoever wrote the proposal. Owns it through review, approval, and build.
- **Members** review proposals, bring student perspective, pick up build work
- **Sponsor** John. Air cover, introductions, unsticking things.

## Idea to shipped code

1. **Raise** a one liner in the backlog
2. **Triage** at a sync. Drop, park, or write up. Most should be dropped or parked.
3. **Write up** using the template
4. **Check what exists.** Roadmap, owning team, community forum. Already planned means we park it.
5. **Team review** with whoever owns the code. Their read decides if this goes further.
6. **Approval** from the owning PM and eng lead
7. **Build** through the normal process. No shortcuts.
8. **Close the loop.** Update the proposal with what actually happened.

## Bar for a proposal

Ready for team review when all of these are true.

- Problem stated in two sentences, no jargon
- Evidence beyond one person's experience
- The what exists today section is filled in and accurate
- Scope says what is out, not just what is in
- The strongest objection is written down
- An engineer on the owning team could argue with specifics

## Rules of thumb

- Evidence beats opinion. Tickets, forum threads, third party workarounds, asking real students.
- Scope down until it fits a term
- If it needs more than a couple of co-ops' spare hours, it is a proposal, not a build
- Write the objection you are most afraid of. Someone will raise it anyway.
- Nothing reaches John before it reaches the team that owns the code.
